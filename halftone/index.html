Two settings that shape a halftone.

01 / DPI

How finely the printer can place dots

DPI means dots per inch. It shows how many tiny dot positions the printer can use in one inch. A higher DPI can make the edges of a halftone dot look smoother.

02 / LPI

How many halftone cells fit in one inch

LPI means lines per inch. It shows how many rows of halftone cells fit in one inch. A higher LPI creates more, smaller cells, so the screen pattern looks finer.

Inside one screen cell

DPI ÷ LPI gives the printer positions along a cell edge. For a simple square cell with an integer ratio, (DPI ÷ LPI)² + 1 gives the theoretical tone levels, including white and solid black.

600 DPI ÷ 60 LPI = 10
10 × 10 = 100 positions
100 + 1 = 101 possible tones
